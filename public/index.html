How it works

Magistrates need specific, clear details to grant a protection order. inFORMed helps you give them, without the conversation ever feeling like a form.

1

Talk at your own pace

Share what happened in your own words and your own language. inFORMed asks one gentle question at a time, and you can stop whenever you like.

2

Watch your form take shape

Each detail goes into the right section of the court form, written the way magistrates need to read it: dates, places, what was said, and how things have got worse.

3

Take it to court

Download your draft as a PDF or Word document and take it to the clerk at a Magistrates' Court. You can check and change anything first.

Good to know

From the guides inFORMed is built on.

1

You can apply for a protection order yourself, for free, without a lawyer and without opening a police case first.

2

You apply at a Magistrates' Court where you live, work or study, where the other person lives or works, or where the abuse happened.Court hours are usually 9am to 3:30pm on weekdays. If you are in danger you can apply after hours.

3

Domestic violence is more than physical harm. It includes emotional, economic and sexual abuse, harassment, intimidation and controlling behaviour.

4

Photos, doctors' notes or witnesses can help, but you can still apply without them. A child of any age can apply too.

About inFORMed

inFORMed is GRIT's AI legal navigation tool. It speaks like a caring community member, never rushes or judges, and helps people experiencing domestic violence put their experience into an application a court can act on.

Its guidance is grounded in the official protection order application form, guides on domestic violence rights and on writing a strong application, MOSAIC's research on what magistrates look for, and GRIT's own protection order guide.

About GRIT

GRIT (Gender Rights in Tech) is a South African feminist technology organisation that uses technology to support survivors of gender-based violence and help people access information, support and resources around their rights, health and wellbeing.

GRIT’s work is grounded in:

  • Survivor-centred and trauma-informed practice, with safety and the needs of survivors at the centre of its technology and programmes.
  • Co-creation with communities, including young women, survivors and people whose experiences inform the design of the tools.
  • Accessible digital support, including Zuzi, the AI-powered chat experience within the GRIT app, alongside WhatsApp-based support and other digital tools.
  • Practical access to safety and support, including features such as the GRIT app’s Panic Button and Evidence Vault.

    What happens next
    1. Take your draft to the clerk at a Magistrates' Court during court hours (usually 9am to 3:30pm on weekdays).
    2. The clerk helps you complete the official form. You sign it in front of a commissioner of oaths.
    3. If the court grants an interim order, it is served on the respondent and you are given a date to return to court.

    Save to finish later

    Your application will be locked with a PIN and kept on this device only. Nobody can open it without the PIN, and it is never sent to GRIT. The conversation itself is not saved.
